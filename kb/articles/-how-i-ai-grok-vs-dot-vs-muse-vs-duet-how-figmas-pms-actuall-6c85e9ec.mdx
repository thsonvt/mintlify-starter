Grok vs. Dot vs. Muse vs. Claude Duet: which one crushes the competition?Listen now on YouTube Spotify Apple PodcastsBrought to you by:WorkOSMake your app enterprise-ready, with SSO, SCIM, RBAC, and moreFirecrawlGive your AI agents web data and beyondIn this solo episode, Claire puts Grok Bot, Dot, Metas Muse, and Claude Duet through nine real-world tasks to find out which personal agents are actually worth using. She tests them on everything from Slack replies, inbox triage, and coding to video editing, Figma thumbnails, family projects, and shopping. The results point to a clear split.Biggest takeaways:Grok Bot is the strongest all-around personal agent in this benchmark, especially for work. It scored 93% overall and won the Figma, inbox triage, and shopping tasks. For someone choosing one agent for professional workflows, Claire recommends starting here.Muse is the best agent for personal life. It created the strongest short-form video and family newspaper with almost no prompting and answered a physics question too quickly to time. That speed has led Claire to move more quick Q&A conversations from ChatGPT to Muse.Speed is central to the product experience. Muse finished first on four of the nine tasks, while Claude Duet was consistently the slowest. When an agent disappears for several minutes, users lose context and stop wanting to use it.Claude Duet asks for permission too often. It requested approval four times during the benchmark, compared with once for every other agent. That caution may be well-intentioned, but it makes Duet feel like something the user must supervise rather than trust.An agent is only as capable as the tools it can access. A Codex audit found that Claires agents did not have equivalent connectors and skills. Muse lacked Slack and GitHub, which prevented it from completing several work tasks, highlighting the importance of equal setups for a fair comparison.Grok Bot is the agent Claire trusts most with Figma. Dot and Claude Duet mostly filled existing templates, while Grok Bot proposed fresh headlines and layouts that looked like real YouTube thumbnails. It has consistently outperformed the others across Claires Figma tests.Claude Duet and Dot still showed the strongest coding judgment. Dot came closest to the intended Sentry fix, while Duet found a second bug and avoided duplicating work Dot had already completed. Claire would still choose Claude or Codex for serious coding, just not through Duets personal-agent interface.Dot showed the strongest personal context in one important moment. It was the only agent that asked whether Claires Lovable talk was paid and remote, which felt like genuine chief-of-staff judgment. Its poor performance on creative tasks, however, kept it well behind Grok Bot and Muse overall.The useful takeaway is that different agents belong in different parts of life. Claires current split is Grok Bot for work and Muse for personal tasks. In this episode, he demonstrates how Figmas PMs use it to manage Slack, gather organizational context, create artifacts, and run more thorough bug bashes with Codex. He also walks through AutoXP, an AI growth PM that runs experiments with a single human checkpoint, and a real-time Jev assistant that improves writing as he types.Biggest takeaways:The best place to start with AI is the work PMs routinely skip. Ezra designed PM OS around tasks that fall through the cracks, such as involving support before a launch or documenting meetings. His goal is to establish a minimum acceptable standard for every PM workflow.Context-gathering skills are the foundation of useful AI at work. At a company with roughly 2,000 employees, even identifying the right collaborators can be difficult. One PM OS skill maps a team and automatically adds the relevant designer, engineering manager, and team leaders as reviewers.A simple whats up skill can make Slack manageable. Ezra runs it about six times a day to gather relevant threads, projects, open decisions, and people awaiting a response. He archives roughly 10 channels daily because the skill preserves what actually matters.A basic Git repository can support an entire PM organization. PM OS consists of an AGENTS.md file, reusable skills, and a few command hooks. An automatic version check keeps 60 to 70 PMs aligned, while private subrepositories give each person room to customize.Successful AI adoption starts with familiar tools, then evolves with users. PM OS began as a terminal interface in Claude Code before gaining a local web interface that was easier to read. Ezra now works primarily in Codex, partly because browser use has become reliable enough for his workflow.Agents can be more diligent bug bashers than humans. Ezra gives his agent a test account and a FigJam board full of scenarios, then has it document its findings. It has caught edge cases ranging from a text box filled with 1,000 emojis to grammar that changed based on a teams name.In growth, wasted traffic is the biggest sin. AutoXP uses three agents to identify funnel drop-off, propose new copy, and prepare the implementation. A human reviews the experiment once before launch, after which the system analyzes results and begins the next iteration automatically.Fast, inexpensive models can provide feedback that feels continuous. Ezras Jev prototype reviews writing whenever the user pauses, flags placeholders and unsupported claims, and finds relevant interview quotes from a research library. The speed makes it feel less like running an AI task and more like working beside an attentive editor.When an AI conversation goes bad, starting over often beats arguing with it. Ezra treats poor output as a sign of unclear instructions or contaminated context.
